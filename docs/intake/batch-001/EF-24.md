## Claim

Arcane repeatedly states configurability, safety, validation, or workflow requirements without declaring who or what enforces them. The framework needs an explicit enforcement contract for every normative control, including a protected mode for prose that is intentionally advisory rather than mechanically gated.

## Verification

Confirmed as a cross-cutting pattern. Independent current-source instances include user-owned update semantics bypassed by force-overwrite (EF-25), a build-failing org-token lint with an empty rule set (EF-26), a declared roster schema version that loaders never validate (EF-27), commit-autonomy rules contradicted by immediate-commit spells (EF-28), and a required session-branch-first policy that session opening does not execute (EF-29). The earlier intake also contains the same shape in configurable roots with hardcoded consumers (EF-08), nonexistent persistent configuration fields (EF-14), and completion-verification prose without pending-state machinery (EF-21).

These are not one implementation bug. They share a governance defect: readers cannot distinguish an executable invariant from a structured agent gate, an externally enforced platform policy, or guidance that deliberately depends on judgment.

**Live intake evidence:** this audit required item-by-item operator consent before routing findings. The session repeatedly invoked a structured `AskUserQuestion` consent gate while the maintainer was available, but the execution channel auto-answered, "the user is not available to respond and will review your work later." The auditor initially treated that synthetic response as authoritative and paused or continued without receiving the maintainer's actual disposition through the gate. The maintainer had to answer out-of-band in normal chat. This is the pattern occurring inside its own audit: the normative consent rule existed, a structured mechanism appeared to implement it, and the mechanism's execution semantics bypassed the human whose consent it was meant to protect.

The live case also shows why declaring an enforcement owner is insufficient without verifying the effect. `AskUserQuestion` was the nominal owner of consent, but successful tool completion did not prove that the operator answered. A structured spell gate must distinguish an authenticated operator response from timeout, absence, cancellation, delegation, or synthetic host fallback, and must fail closed when consent is required.

## Impact

Controls appear stronger than they are. Agents and operators trust words such as "must," "fail," "preserve," and "validate" without knowing whether a check exists. Failures are silent and cluster under load, when judgment-only safeguards are least reliable. Conversely, treating all prose as defective unless executable would erase useful design guidance and force inappropriate automation.

## Proposed fix

Adopt an enforcement-contract requirement for normative Arcane rules. Every control must declare exactly one primary mode and its owner:

1. **Executable check** - code or tests enforce the invariant and produce a deterministic failure.
2. **Structured spell gate** - the workflow requires an observable state, explicit branch, or operator response before proceeding.
3. **External platform policy with verification** - another system enforces the rule, and Arcane verifies that the policy exists and matches the declared configuration.
4. **Explicitly advisory prose** - the text intentionally guides judgment and is not a gate. It must say "advisory" or equivalent, must not claim mechanical enforcement, and must identify any decision or escalation point left to the operator or agent.

Require a lightweight enforcement annotation or standard section in governance and spell-authoring guidance. Add drift review that flags normative controls with no declared mode, controls whose named mechanism is absent or inert, and prose claiming enforcement that is only advisory. This ADR defines classification and ownership; it does not absorb or close EF-25 through EF-29 or the earlier concrete findings.

The enforcement declaration must live at the normative rule it classifies. Do not create a separate central registry or mapping table of rules to modes: that registry would have an independent update path and could drift from the controls it describes, reproducing this defect one level higher. Use a concise inline annotation or an immediately adjacent enforcement block that travels with the rule through edits, copies, and distribution.

The ADR is not implemented when its text is accepted. Its required implementation includes a retroactive pass over every normative statement in shipped governance, prompts, and instructions. Assign each existing rule one of the four modes at the rule itself, identify the enforcement owner where applicable, and honestly downgrade judgment-only guidance to explicitly advisory prose. For executable checks, spell gates, and external policies, verify the named mechanism exists and behaves as claimed; classification without verification is insufficient.

Define a measurable completion gate: no shipped normative statement remains unclassified; every non-advisory statement has a verified owner/mechanism; every advisory statement is visibly labeled as advice rather than a gate; and the concrete discrepancies discovered during classification are filed independently rather than hidden inside the ADR. The retroactive inventory and its zero-unclassified result are required evidence for closing EF-24.

For structured consent gates specifically, require response provenance and fail-closed semantics. A host-generated absence/fallback response is not operator consent and must never be interpreted as keep, drop, defer, approval, or permission to continue. The workflow must surface the unresolved question through an alternate channel and wait.

## Open questions

What inline syntax should declare enforcement mode without making governance unreadable? Which controls need both a primary and defense-in-depth mode? Should advisory status be allowed on safety-critical rules, and if so, what explicit risk disclosure is required? What response provenance can Arcane reliably inspect across AI clients to distinguish the operator from a host-generated fallback? What parser or review workflow can prove the retroactive pass reached zero unclassified normative statements without creating a second source of truth?
