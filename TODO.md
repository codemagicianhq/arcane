Approval on one track does **not** promote the binary anywhere: production is a separate track needing its own release **and its own country list**. Batched clicks land on the wrong questions and can silently set things like graphic violence or location sharing to *Yes*. Answer one question at a time and re-read the form after any category change.

  **Cross-platform lessons that belong in the shared preamble (both stores):**
  - **Never ship a native rebuild while a submission is in review** just to carry a JS-only change — it can cost the queue position. Hold the change and ship it as an OTA after the verdict.
  - **A reviewer's FIRST cold launch must already be correct**, so a review build must carry the work natively rather than relying on an update being fetched. This is the direct consequence of the two-launch rule below.
  - **OTA updates apply on the NEXT launch, not the current one** — the client downloads in the background and swaps on relaunch. Do not diagnose a "stuck" update until two full quit-and-reopen cycles.
  - **🔴 Bundler transform caches do not key on `EXPO_PUBLIC_*` env vars.** On a persistent (self-hosted) runner the cache survives between runs, so an update published to one channel can ship another channel's inlined config — e.g. a production build pointing at the development API. Pass `--clear-cache` on the publish lane, and **verify by comparing the published `launchAsset` hashes across channels**: identical hashes for two channels that differ by at least one env var is proof of the defect. Pipeline-green is not evidence here.
  - **When a feature is removed or renamed, both stores' screenshots and descriptions are inside the blast radius.** Store metadata outlives the code it depicts and is the first thing a reviewer opens.
